# `analysis` — Phase E: the limit cycle and its neighborhood

## Summary

Phase E's analysis layer, one function per commit after a literature survey (roadmap,
ADR-0012). This commit is **E1**: `analysis.Cycle`, the shared representation of a planar
limit cycle (a Fourier closed curve in a phase uniform in time, with its period), and
`analysis.data.estimate_cycle`, which finds the cycle from one or several sampled,
possibly noisy trajectories using only their tails — no settling time needed — and reports
whether the tail was on the cycle. On the normal forms it recovers radius, center, period
and phase to $10^{-8}$; on FitzHugh–Nagumo it gives Langfield et al.'s period to
$2\cdot10^{-5}$ and the clockwise orientation; with measurement noise the error shrinks
with the sample. The survey, method, oracles and limits are `docs/design/analysis.md` §1.

## Design

ADR-0012 has the layout decisions. Points specific to E1 (details and numbers in the
design document §1.2):

- **Polar angle as protophase, Kralemann's PTP as the phase.** The literature's hard
  step — a protophase from a scalar signal — does not arise when the planar state is
  observed; the density correction that turns a protophase into a phase uniform in time
  carries over unchanged and is credited.
- **The density is a time integral over exactly `tail_periods` revolutions, not a sample
  mean.** A sample count off by one at the window edge biases every Fourier coefficient
  by $1/n$; on the Bautin oracle that was a $5\cdot10^{-3}$ phase error, now $2\cdot10^{-7}$.
- **Period from the slope of the unwrapped phase**, pooled over trajectories with one
  intercept each, instead of the median of crossing intervals (kept as `period_samples`
  for diagnosis): with noise $\sigma = 0.05$ the error drops from $3\cdot10^{-3}$ to
  $2\cdot10^{-5}$ (Zielinski et al. 2014: estimators that use every sample are the robust
  ones).
- **`converged` compares the earlier tail revolutions against the curve fitted to the
  last revolution alone.** Comparing a whole-tail fit with itself hides the transient in
  the averaged curve (ratio 1.1 where the radius changes by 0.3; the last-revolution
  reference gives 2.9).
- **Defaults**: `harmonics=11` (Revzen & Guckenheimer), `density_harmonics=2·11+2`
  (cheap; its truncation is the phase's error), `tail_periods=3`, `tolerance=2`.
  FitzHugh–Nagumo wants `harmonics=24` (documented in the docstring and the test).

## 2026-10-18 — O1: `analysis.ode.limit_cycle` (ADR-0014)

### Summary

The periodic orbit and period of a planar `AbstractODE`, from the vector field: settle,
a cross-section normal to the flow, the first-return map iterated to its fixed point (the
minimum-period test as period-$k$ detection), then multiple shooting on the time-$T$ flow
with forward-mode AD Jacobians and Levenberg–Marquardt, coarsening to $m = 1$; guards
against the two extraneous solutions of shooting; the monodromy's trivial-multiplier error
as the accuracy readout; `reverse_time` to find repelling cycles. Refactored from Joon's
procedural prototype (`prototype_analysis.ipynb`, `438c944`) after the O1 survey (design
document §2) and the decisions of 2026-10-17/18 (ADR-0014). No change to `analysis.data`.

### Design

- Shooting on the time-$T$ flow rather than collocation; flow-normal Poincaré section
  as the phase condition; `PoincareMap` as the one abstraction; frozen dataclasses for
  results; reverse time for repelling cycles; Liouville and the multiplier's
  interpretation deferred to O2 — all in ADR-0014 with the rejected alternatives.
- Review items from the prototype that shaped the code: the solver tolerance is the
  residual floor (the prototype at rtol 1e-6 had a 1e-6 residual at the *guess*, so LM
  could not act) → `SHOOTING_SOLVER_CONFIG` at rtol 1e-10; the two-solve crossing scheme
  could lock onto the wrong intersection of a non-convex cycle → the fixed-point loop
  detects period-$k$ orbits of the map; no horizon → `t_max` with `NotConvergedError`; a
  one-shot settle check → iteration with a contraction record; unnormalized section
  normal → unit normal; no guards or diagnostics → equilibrium and $T \to 0$ guards,
  monodromy and residual; output sampled from the guess → from the solution.
- Two diffrax findings (ADR-0014 §6): a zero of the event condition at $t_0$ counts as
  a crossing, and a reverse-time solve hands the condition its internal increasing
  time — hence the departure solve instead of a time guard.

## Review round 1 (2026-10-18) — O1

Joon's review (`fbe0c91`) and the discussion that followed; four decisions were his
(jit scope, custom JVP now, drop coarsening, functions not a class), the rest
uncontroversial.

- **Names**: `Hyperplane` → `CrossSection` (the dynamical-systems term; Parker & Chua
  §2.1.2 added to the credits), `through` → `normal_to_flow`, `__call__` →
  `signed_distance`; `limit_cycle` → `find_limit_cycle`.
- **Jit scope**: `PoincareMap.__call__` and `iterate` (now a `lax.scan`) are jit- and
  vmap-compatible; a missing crossing is an `EquinoxRuntimeError` (`throw=True`, as in
  diffrax) or `tau = inf` (`throw=False`); `_return_solve` and `_event_direction` became
  the module-level differentiable core `_first_return` and the property
  `event_direction`. `fixed_point` and `find_limit_cycle` stay host-side drivers (a
  jitted settle would need a bounded history and a masked minimal-period search for a
  stage that runs once; to be revisited if a use case asks). `multiple_shooting` is
  jittable (one optimistix solve; `ShootingResult` is a `NamedTuple` of arrays).
- **Custom JVP**: the map is differentiable in the point through `eqx.filter_custom_jvp`
  — Eq. 5.21 for the point, $d\tau = -\langle n, J\thinspace dx\rangle/\langle n, v\rangle$ for the
  time (implicit differentiation of the crossing); `jacobian` removed, `jax.jacfwd(P)`
  replaces it. Differentiation with respect to the ODE's parameters or the section is
  `NotImplementedError`.
- **No coarsening**: `multiple_shooting` solves at the given $m$ only.
- **`AbstractODE.flow_to`** replaces `_flow_to`, `segment_flow` and `_resample`. Found on the way: a normal form's `flow` is its *closed form* by default, so routing the analysis through `ode.flow` made the normal-form tests circular and broke the reverse-time Winfree case at the basin edge (`nan` at $r = a$, $10^{-7}$ in the period just inside). Round 1 answered this with an always-numerical `AbstractODE.integrate` — reverted in round 2 (below) in favor of the normal forms' own integration switch;
  `time_sign` arguments became `reverse_time: bool`; `shooting_residual` is typed
  (`ShootingArgs`).
- **Stages as functions**: `settle`, `initial_guess`, `polish`, `is_equilibrium`,
  `period_consistent`, `sample_orbit`, `orientation`, all exported; `find_limit_cycle`
  composes them.
- **Monodromy out of `LimitCycle`**: the orbit's properties are O2's functions of a
  `LimitCycle`; the tests that used the monodromy now use `jax.jacfwd(P)`.
- **`LevelSetCycle` → `systems/level_set.py`** with `guckenheimer_meloon()` and
  `cassini()`; `tests/helpers.py` keeps only the tolerances.
- `SHOOTING_SOLVER_CONFIG`: `Dopri8` justified in the docstring (eighth order at
  $10^{-10}$–$10^{-12}$ tolerances); `max_steps=65536`.
- `with_adjoint` kept as the one-line helper (`copy.replace`, Joon's edit).
- Tests added: the map under jit and vmap; $d\tau$ against finite differences;
  `multiple_shooting` under jit; the reversed map's eigenvalue $e^{-\kappa_u T}$;
  `throw=False` returns `inf`. Tolerances recalibrated to $10^{-8}$ (the first round's
  $10^{-9}$ had been measured on the closed-form flow; the solver's global error over a
  period at rtol $10^{-10}$ is $\sim 10^{-9}$). The design document's split into one file per item is
  deferred until §3 lands.

## 2026-10-20 — O2: `analysis.ode.floquet` (ADR-0015), `LimitCycle` as a module

### Summary

The Floquet multipliers and exponents of a `LimitCycle` by the periodic Schur chain on the shooting-segment Jacobians, seeded with the flow direction and run in the direction in which the cycle attracts — the refactoring of Joon's prototype (`35396aa`) with the direction and sign fixes it needed — plus the explicit monodromy and Liouville's divergence integral as checks and the return-map eigenvalue as a diagnostic. `LimitCycle` and `FixedPoint` become `eqx.Module`s; the cycle carries its ODE and solver configuration and samples itself; analysis functions take the cycle. The stage kernels are jitted.

### Design

- The seeded periodic Schur chain as the method, forward, in the attracting direction; Liouville and the explicit monodromy as checks; `Literal` dispatch; `LimitCycle` as a module carrying its ODE — all in ADR-0015 with the rejected alternatives (Liouville as the method, the trace of $DP$, a general periodic QR library, strategy objects now, a stored dense interpolant).
- The kernels take arrays, the drivers take the cycle: a `LimitCycle` holds the ragged `settle` record, so a jitted function taking the cycle would recompile per cycle.
- `multipliers_from_monodromy` orders the eigenvalue closest to 1 first (not by modulus), so index 0 is the trivial multiplier for attracting and repelling cycles alike.

### Bugs fixed (in the prototype, before it became code)

- The sign normalization scaled the *columns* of $R$ (`R * s`), which keeps the diagonal right and the factorization wrong whenever the two signs differ; rows of $R$ and columns of $Q$ must flip together. Pinned by `test_periodic_schur_factorization_is_exact_and_vmappable`.
- The chain was run backward by RQ from $Z_K = Q_1$ (Bojanczyk's proof order), which propagates the subdominant Schur vector and does not close ($\Vert Z_m - Q_1\Vert = 0.63$ on FHN, multipliers $[8\cdot10^{-3}, 2\cdot10^{-10}]$ for $[1, 1.7\cdot10^{-12}]$); the forward QR chain closes to $10^{-10}$.
- The trace of the return-map Jacobian ($3.5\cdot10^{-9}$ for $\mu = 1.7\cdot10^{-12}$): not a bug in code, a wrong estimator in the survey (mine); `return_map_multiplier` takes the eigenvalue and the FHN test asserts the leakage.
- `limit_cycle.py`'s docstring claimed every stage was jitted; none was. They are now.
