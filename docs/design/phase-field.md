## 1. The problem

Conventions fixed with Joon up front: the public phase is a fraction of the period, $\Theta \in [0, 1)$, as in `model/base.py`, with $\Theta = 0$ at `cycle.x0s[0]` and increasing with time, $\Theta(\gamma(t)) = t/T$ and $\Theta(\Phi_t x) = \Theta(x) + t/T \bmod 1$; the difference of two phases is the signed shortest arc in $(-\tfrac12, \tfrac12]$, taken by `phase_difference`; everything is computed in time along `LimitCycle.sample` and rescaled by $1/T$ at the end; the per-point function is vmappable and a `phase_grid` utility handles the grid (masking, chunking). The convention changed on 2026-10-30 under ADR-0020; before, the phase was an angle in $(-\pi, \pi]$. Where a cited source writes the phase as an angle, its form is kept and ours is given beside it.

### 1.1 O3 — the phase field of the basin

Given a `LimitCycle` of a planar `AbstractODE`, compute the asymptotic phase $\Theta(x)$ of any $x$ in the basin — Winfree's definition, $\lim_{t\to\infty}\vert\Phi_t(x) - \Phi_{t + \Theta(x)T}(x_0)\vert = 0$ (Winfree 1974; Langfield et al. 2014 §II.A, [cited]) — to the accuracy of the orbit, with `nan` outside the basin and on the phaseless set, and cheaply enough for a grid. This is the field a learned model's phase is compared against (roadmap M; Yawata et al. 2024 Figs. 4 and 8 compare on exactly such a grid, [cited]).

### 1.2 O4 — the phase response curve on the cycle

$Z(\Theta) = \nabla\Theta(\gamma(\Theta))$ at the cycle point $\gamma(\Theta)$ of phase $\Theta$, the infinitesimal phase response curve (phase sensitivity function), normalized by $\langle Z, f\rangle = 1/T$ (ADR-0020 decision 4). The sources write the phase as an angle and normalize by $\langle Z, f\rangle = \omega$, $\omega = 2\pi/T$ (Brown et al. 2004 Eq. 2.4/A.6; Nakao 2016 Eqs. 12–13 with $Z\cdot dX_0/d\theta = 1$, the same normalization in angle, [cited]); in turns both are $\langle Z, f\rangle = 1/T$. $Z$ is what enters every phase reduction, $\dot\theta = \omega + Z(\theta)\cdot G$ in the sources' angle and $\dot\Theta = 1/T + Z(\Theta)\cdot G$ in turns, and the second thing a learned model's phase is assessed on (Yawata et al. Figs. 5, 9, 12, 15: estimated vs "true PSF calculated by solving the adjoint equation", [cited]).

## 2. What exists

### 2.1 The routes to the phase field

**The object.** Isochrons are the level sets of $\Theta$; Guckenheimer 1975 (Theorem A, Appendix A, [cited]) shows each isochron $I(c)$ is the stable manifold of $c \in \gamma$ under the time-$T$ map $\Phi_T$, a cross-section of $\gamma$ diffeomorphic to $\mathbb R^{n-1}$, with the isochrons permuted by the flow ($I(\gamma(t)) = \Phi_t(I(\gamma(0)))$) and their union the basin. Two consequences we use: $D\Phi_T(c)$ has the eigenvalue $1$ along $f(c)$ and the stable eigenvalues transverse to it, and "the Stable Manifold Theorem ensures that $I(c)$ is tangent at $c$ to the stable linear eigenspace $E^s(c)$ that is spanned by the $n - 1$ stable eigenvectors of $D\Phi_T(c)$" (Langfield et al. 2014 §II.B, [cited]) — so in the plane the isochron through a mesh point is tangent to O2's Floquet vector $v(c)$; and the phaseless set is the frontier of the basin (Guckenheimer Appendix C: for planar flows every point of the frontier is phaseless, Theorem C1, [cited]) — FitzHugh–Nagumo's source equilibrium, Winfree's hole $r \leq a$. Isochrons accumulate on the phaseless set, and for slow–fast systems also in an *almost phaseless* region along the repelling slow manifold, where the phase is "very sensitive, meaning that a slight perturbation from any point will result in a completely different phase" (Langfield et al. §IV, [cited]; Osinga & Moehlis 2010 §4.4 "almost phaseless set", [cited]). No method resolves the phase there; the grid utility must report it, not hide it.

**Routes to $\Theta(x)$.**

| Route | Computes | Where it stands | Cost / accuracy |
|---|---|---|---|
| (a) Forward integration, phase read off the cycle | $\Phi_{nT}(x)$ for $n$ periods, then the phase of the cycle point it lands beside | Winfree's original computation: "asymptotic phase from forward-time integrations", which "was not accurate enough to resolve the isochron structure" in the sensitive regions (Langfield et al. §I, §III, [cited]); Monga et al. 2019 Eq. | (d) Backward integration of a local isochron | extend the linear isochron $E^s(c)$ by the reversed flow | Sherwood & Guckenheimer 2010; Guillamon & Huguet 2009 [cited via Langfield et al. 2014, §I and Osinga & Moehlis 2010, §3]; Winfree's second method | gives *curves*, not a field; "requires exponentially small variations in the initial condition", impossible in double precision for slow–fast systems (Osinga & Moehlis §3, [cited]) |
| (e) BVP continuation | orbit segments ending on $E^s(c)$, continued in AUTO | Osinga & Moehlis 2010 (SIADS 9:1201); Langfield et al. 2014 (the FHN isochrons, "Winfree's puzzle") [cited] | the method of record for isochron *geometry* in sensitive regions; a continuation code, not a field evaluator |
| (f) Parametrization method | $K(\theta, \sigma)$ with $\partial_\theta K/T + \lambda\sigma\partial_\sigma K = X(K)$ (Eq. 9), the conjugacy of the basin to $\dot\theta = 1/T$, $\dot\sigma = \lambda\sigma$ (Eq. 10); $\Theta(x) = \theta$ and $\Sigma(x) = \sigma$ where $x = K(\theta, \sigma)$ (Eqs. 13, 15); $\nabla\Theta, \nabla\Sigma$ as the rows of $[DK]^{-1}$ (Eq. 23) | Guillamon & Huguet 2009 (SIADS 8:1005), Huguet & de la Llave 2013 (SIADS 12:1763) for planar systems; Pérez-Cervera et al. 2020 (Chaos 30:083117) in $d \geq 3$ ([cited]; see §2.2) | $K$ as a Fourier–Taylor series to order $L$ in $\sigma$, valid on a domain $\Omega_{\rm loc}$ where the invariance residual is $< E_{\rm tol}$ (their §III.C); $\Theta$ anywhere by flowing *into* $\Omega_{\rm loc}$ and inverting $K$ ([deduced] from Eq. 12 — their globalization runs the other way, backward, to draw curves); the same $K$ gives $\Sigma$ (O5) and both response functions (O4, O6): the shared engine of §2.2 |
| (g) Eikonal / level-set PDE | $\nabla\Theta\cdot f = \omega$ solved on a grid (in turns, $\nabla\Theta\cdot f = 1/T$) | Detrixhe et al. 2016 [cited via Pérez-Cervera et al. 2020] | grid-native; the PDE has infinitely many solutions without the asymptotic condition (Brown et al. 2004 §3.2.2, [cited]) |

**What the learned-model comparison needs** is a field on a grid with the sensitive regions marked, not isochron curves: (a)/(b) with $n$ chosen from $\mu$, vmapped. The normal forms give the field in closed form (`phase`, with its $\Psi(r)$ shift for $\omega(r) \neq$ const); FHN's truth is (a) run to convergence, which is what Yawata et al. did and what Langfield et al. show qualitatively (their Figs. 3–4: the isochrons, the spiral accumulation at $q$, the boomerang turns near the repelling slow manifold).

### 2.2 The parametrization method as the shared engine for O3–O6 (Pérez-Cervera et al. 2020, read in full)

**What it is.** $K(\theta, \sigma)$ conjugates the basin to the linear flow $\dot\theta = 1/T$, $\dot\sigma = \lambda\sigma$ (Eqs. 9–10): $\Phi_t(K(\theta, \sigma)) = K(\theta + t/T, e^{\lambda t}\sigma)$ (Eq. 12). In the plane $\sigma$ is one amplitude and $\lambda = \kappa$ (their $\lambda_1$ is O2's exponent). $K$ is sought as $\sum_m K_m(\theta)\sigma^m$ (Eq. 27) with 1-periodic coefficients held as $N$ Fourier modes. Their §III.B, step by step, against what O1–O2 already provide:

| Step | Their computation | Ours |
|---|---|---|
| $m = 0$ | $K_0 = \gamma$: Poincaré section + Newton, then the orbit and the variational matrix $\Phi(\theta T)$ stored at $N$ equispaced $\theta$ (FFT between real and Fourier space) | **done**: `find_limit_cycle`, `LimitCycle.sample(N)`; $\Phi(t_j)$ by `jacfwd(flow_to)` per sample (or O2's factors chained) |
| $m = 1$ | $K_1(\theta) = \Phi(\theta T)\thinspace e^{-\lambda\theta T}\thinspace v$, $v$ the Floquet vector of the monodromy (Eq. 30); the scale $b$ of $v$ is free and sets the series' numerical range (Remark 3.2, §III.C, Table II) | **done up to the scale**: `floquet(cycle).vectors` is $K_1$ at the mesh points; between them, transport as in §2.4(e) |
| $m \geq 2$ | the homological equations (31): $\frac1T K_m' + m\lambda K_m = DX(K_0)K_m + B_m$, with $B_m$ the degree-$m$ Taylor coefficient of $X(K(\theta, \sigma))$ in $\sigma$, obtained by *Taylor-series automatic differentiation* (Appendix B); solved through the Floquet normal form $\Phi(t) = Q(t)e^{tR}$ (§III.A.1, Appendix A), which makes the equation diagonal in Fourier space, $u_k^{(j)} = A_k^{(j)}/(2\pi ik/T + m\lambda - \lambda_{j-1})$ (Eq. 43) | **not done; every ingredient exists in the stack**: $B_m$ is `jax.experimental.jet` (truncated Taylor-mode AD of $X$ along the one-dimensional series $K(\theta, \cdot)$ — exactly their Appendix B, in the plane the univariate case `jet` covers); $Q(\theta)$ from $\Phi(\theta T)$ and O2's $(1, \mu)$ by Eq. A4; the Fourier solve is `jnp.fft` and one division; the non-resonance condition (Remark 3.3) is automatic in the plane ($\lambda < 0$, $m \geq 2$) |
| accuracy and domain | $N$ by the tail criterion (47), $L$ and $b$ by experiment so that the monomials stay $O(1)$; $\Omega_{\rm loc}$ by the residual $E(\theta, \sigma)$ of the invariance equation (48–49) | the residual is one `jet`/`jacfwd` evaluation of Eq. 9 on a $(\theta, \sigma)$ grid |
| inversion | not needed for their curves (they evaluate $K$) | $\Theta(x), \Sigma(x)$ by a $2\times2$ Newton on $K(\theta, \sigma) = x$ inside $\Omega_{\rm loc}$ (optimistix), seeded by the nearest sample and $\sigma = 0$ |
| globalization | backward from $\Omega_{\rm loc}$, along the slow manifold, to draw isochrons and isostables (Algorithms 3.5, 3.8); response functions carried by the adjoint equations (24) | for a *field* on a grid, forward instead: flow $x$ for $nT$ until $\Phi_{nT}(x) \in \Omega_{\rm loc}$ (one period for FHN), invert $K$ there, then $\Theta(x) = \Theta(\Phi_{nT}x)$, $\Sigma(x) = e^{-\lambda nT}\Sigma(\Phi_{nT}x)$ (Eq. 12 read backward; [deduced]) — the read-off error is $O(\sigma^{L+1})$ instead of route (a)'s $O(\sigma)$, so $L$ trades against $n$ |

**Effort.** With `jet`, `fft`, `jacfwd` and optimistix in hand, the planar $K$ to order $L$ is one module of roughly the size of `floquet.py`: the homological solve is a dozen lines once $Q(\theta)$ is formed; the engineering is in the three tuning knobs ($N$, $L$, $b$) and in the residual-based $\Omega_{\rm loc}$ test, which they set by experiment (§III.C, Table II: for their slow–fast models the monomials decay to zero by degree 5, so $L > 5$ adds nothing and $\Omega_{\rm loc}$ stays thin — expect the same for FHN, where forward flow into $\Omega_{\rm loc}$ then does most of the work anyway; for a near-Hopf Bautin cycle the series is the win). Their timing: $L = 10$, $N = 2048$ in about 10 s on a laptop. A realistic split: $L = 1$ now (O3's (b′): everything is already computed), the series engine as its own item between O4 and O5, since O5/O6 are where it pays twice.

**Why it is the right long-term engine.** One object yields $\Theta$ (O3), $\nabla\Theta$ (O4), $\Sigma$ (O5), $\nabla\Sigma$ (O6) and the isochron/isostable curves — all from one conjugacy $K$, which is also exactly what the learned INN is supposed to be: the comparison of the trained model with $K$ is the comparison of two conjugacies to the same linear normal form, not of a model with a stack of separately computed curves. The alternatives for the later items, for the record: Koopman/Laplace averages for $\Sigma$ (Mauroy & Mezić 2018, Chaos 28:073108 §III: forward integration only, $O(1/\tau)$ convergence, and the amplitude eigenfunction needs the Floquet exponent fed in); the direct exponential fit of the distance to the cycle (roadmap O5's first plan: cheap, $O(\sigma^2)$-biased by the isochron geometry); the return-map eigencoordinate of Wilson & Moehlis 2016 Eq. 5 (first order in the distance to the $\theta = 0$ isochron, i.e. $L = 1$ again); the Eikonal solver of Detrixhe et al. 2016 for isochron fields in high dimension (grid PDE, not planar-specific). None of them gives the response functions off the cycle; (f) does.

**On the worry that direct integration is noisy.** The error of route (b) at a basin point is the solver's, not random: the return times carry the solver's global error ($\sim10^{-9}$ at the shooting tolerances), the event location is Newton-polished, and the remainder after $n$ returns is $O(\mu^n)$; the result is a smooth function of $x$ to $\sim 2\cdot10^{-9}$ in turns ($\sim10^{-8}$ in angle), as the O1 tests show for the return map itself. What *looks* like noise on a grid is real: in the almost-phaseless regions the true $\Theta$ varies by a whole period, $1$ in turns, across a grid cell, and no method — (e) included, whose authors compute the isochron *curves* there precisely because the field is unresolvable (Langfield et al. §IV) — turns that into a smooth picture. Route (f) does not change this (its $\Omega_{\rm loc}$ excludes those regions, and flowing into it is the same integration); what it changes is the read-off accuracy near the cycle and the number of periods needed when $\mu$ is close to 1.

Done to guard against lock-in; what each adds, and whether it changes the plan.

| Approach | Reference | What it is | Bearing on us |

**Verdict.** Nothing displaces the plan. Three things are adopted from the sweep: Detrixhe et al.'s almost-phaseless-set criterion $\vert\nabla\Theta\vert > \tau$ as `phase_grid`'s mask-and-flag diagnostic; the PPV literature as the citation for computing $Z$ from the shooting structure rather than an eigenanalysis; and Castelli et al.'s spectral $Q(\theta)$ as the fallback for §2.2. One thing is a caution: Detrixhe's first-order field is what a PDE-on-a-grid approach buys, so the field must come from integration (or from $K$), with the grid only as the sampling.

### 2.4 The routes to the PRC

| Route | Computes | Where it stands | Cost / accuracy |
|---|---|---|---|
| (a) Direct (finite perturbation) | $\Delta\Theta/\Delta x$ for a small kick at $\gamma(\theta)$, measured after the transient | Winfree; Brown et al. §2.2.1 [cited]: "the difference $\Delta\theta$ is preserved under the flow", so measure it as $t \to \infty$; Monga et al. §2.1 Fig. 1 compares it with the adjoint [cited] | $O(\Delta x)$ bias, one O3 evaluation per kick; the sanity check every paper shows |
| (b) Adjoint equation | $\dot Z = -Df(\gamma(t))^{\top}Z$, the $T$-periodic solution with $\langle Z(0), f(\gamma(0))\rangle = 1/T$ ($\omega$ in the sources' angle) | Ermentrout & Kopell 1991; Hoppensteadt & Izhikevich 1997 Eqs. 9.16–17; Brown et al. App. A, Eqs. A.4–A.7 ([cited]: $\nabla\theta = \omega Q_{\rm XPP}$); Monga et al. Eqs. 10–11 [cited]; Izhikevich 2007 ch. 10 (Malkin's theorem) [cited via citing papers] | XPP integrates it *backward*. Nakao 2016 (Contemp. Phys. 57:188, Appendix 5, [cited]): forward in time the adjoint equation "is linearly unstable … because of the minus sign before $J^{\dagger}$", the periodic solution "is a neutrally stable mode and therefore easily buried in other unstable modes that grow quickly", whereas backward "only the neutrally stable periodic mode remains, because all other modes are now linearly stable and decay quickly" — "by recording the oscillator state $X_0(\theta)$ on $\chi$ for one period of oscillation, and by evolving the adjoint equation backward in time using the recorded oscillator state for a long time, $Z(\theta)$ can be obtained" (his Fig. E1 is a C program doing exactly this for FHN). | (c) Left eigenvector of the monodromy | $Z(c)^{\top}M(c) = Z(c)^{\top}$, i.e. $Z(c)$ is the left eigenvector of $D\Phi_T(c)$ for the eigenvalue $1$ | $\Theta\circ\Phi_T = \Theta$ differentiated [deduced]; Kvalheim & Revzen 2021 Prop. 5 for the eigenfunction $e^{2\pi i\Theta}$ with exponent $i\omega$, $\omega = 2\pi/T$ [cited]; Osinga & Moehlis §2.1: "in higher dimensions … the left eigenvector for the eigenvalue 1, found by the power method" [cited] | one linear-algebra step per point once $M(c)$ is known — and O2's periodic Schur chain *has* $M(c)$ in triangular form at every mesh point: $Z \propto Q_i\thinspace(1, T_{01}/(T_{00} - T_{11}))$ from the cyclic product $T_i$ of the factors ([deduced]; the planar triangular left eigenproblem) |

| (d) AD of the phase function | $\nabla\Theta(x)$ by `jax.grad` of `asymptotic_phase` through the custom JVP of the return map | Brown et al. §2.2.2: the adjoint "is equivalent to the direct method" [cited]; AD makes the direct method exact in $\Delta x$ [deduced]; Pérez-Cervera et al. Eq. 23: $\nabla\Theta$ as a row of $[DK]^{-1}$ — the gradient of the inverse parametrization [cited] | one tangent per point, on *or off* the cycle (the phase response *function* of Pérez-Cervera et al., Eq. 20–21); its accuracy is O3's |
| (e) Transport along the cycle | $Z(\Phi_t c) = D\Phi_t(c)^{-\top}Z(c)$ | $Z(x)^{\top} = Z(\Phi_t x)^{\top}D\Phi_t(x)$ from $\Theta\circ\Phi_t = \Theta + t/T$ ([deduced]; the integrated form of (b)) | forward solves only; a $2\times2$ solve per point; conditioning $e^{\vert\kappa\vert t}$ — fine from the nearest mesh point ($t \leq T/m$), useless over a full period ($1/\mu$) |

**Conventions.** $Z\cdot f = 1/T$ fixes the scale, and $\Theta = 0$ at $x_0$. Brown et al. fix $Z\cdot f = \omega$ for their angle (A.6), and XPP's $Q$ is their $Z/\omega$ (A.7), so our $Z$ in turns is $Q/T$; sign conventions of the components follow the state coordinates (Yawata's $Z_1, Z_2$). For the Stuart–Landau/Hopf form with shear $\beta$, $Z(\theta) = (-\sin\theta - \beta\cos\theta,\ \cos\theta - \beta\sin\theta)$ (Yawata et al. Eq. 31, [cited], for their phase $\theta$, an angle; divided by $2\pi$ for the phase in turns) — the radial component is the shear; our Bautin and Winfree have $\omega(r) \neq$ const and hence a nonzero radial PRC, Hopf's is tangential.

## 3. The method

**Status:** route (a) of §3.1 is implemented (branch `phase-field`, 2026-10-28); the rest of §3.1 and §3.2 are proposals delivered before implementation (ADR-0012 amendment), and the decisions marked ▶ are Joon's.

### 3.1 The phase field (`analysis/ode/phase.py`)

**Route (a) landed first (2026-10-28, branch `phase-field`).** The presentation of 2026-10-29 needed the fields of fhn and fhn-easy before the method below was settled, so `analysis/ode/phase.py` implements route (a): `asymptotic_phase(cycle, x, *, t_end)` integrates for `t_end`, reads the phase off the cycle at $t_{\rm end}$ and again at $t_{\rm end} + T$ by `nearest_cycle_time`, and reports the gap between the two readings and the final distance from the cycle; `phase_grid` masks the points where either exceeds $10^{-6}$ (**[code]**). The accuracy is set by the solver, about $10^{-9}$ at rtol $10^{-10}$ (`tests/test_phase.py`). The proposal below, with its correction term and the ledger of §3.3, remains the plan for O3 proper; the change document [`2026-10-28-phase-field.md`](../changes/2026-10-28-phase-field.md) records the decisions.

- `asymptotic_phase(cycle, x, *, n_returns=None, t_max=None, throw=False) -> Θ`: route (b′) — which is route (f) truncated at $L = 1$ (§2.2), so the function can later take a higher-order $K$ without changing its contract. The map is `PoincareMap(cycle.ode, cycle.section, cycle.config, t_max, reverse_time=cycle.reverse_time)`; $n$ returns by `iterate` (static $n$, so a `lax.scan`; default from $\mu$: $n = \lceil\log(\varepsilon_{\rm tol}/d_0)/\log\mu\rceil$ with $d_0$ a basin-scale guess, capped), then $\Theta = -\sum_i(\tau_i - T)/T + \langle Z(p), y_n - p\rangle$, reduced mod 1; `nan` where a return is missing (`throw=False`, matching `PoincareMap`). Vmappable; jit left to the caller. ▶ The correction term ties O3 to O4 (`phase_sensitivity` at $p$ is one number per cycle, computed once); without it the function is (b), one order slower in $n$ — include or not?
- `phase_grid(cycle, xs, ys, *, batch_size, **kw) -> (Θ (ny, nx), mask)`: builds the grid, pre-masks by `ode.in_basin`, runs `asymptotic_phase` through `jax.lax.map(..., batch_size=)` over the flattened points (chunked vmap; the solves per point are the cost, $\sim 4\cdot10^4$ for $200\times200$), masks non-finite results, returns the field and the mask. ▶ Its home: `analysis.ode` (the field is an analysis product) or next to the plot code.
- **Reverse time**: for a repelling cycle the phase field of the *reversed* flow is what exists (the forward basin is the complement); `reverse_time` cycles get the reversed map and $\Theta$ of the reversed flow, documented.
- **Sensitivity diagnostic**: $\Vert\nabla\Theta(x)\Vert$ by `jax.grad` through the custom JVP, one tangent per point; `phase_grid` flags the almost-phaseless set $\lbrace\vert\nabla\Theta\vert > \tau\rbrace$ (Detrixhe et al. 2016's definition, §2.3). ▶ on by default, or on request (it doubles the cost of a grid).
- Rejected for now: (c) Fourier averages ($O(1/\tau)$; only wins in high dimension), (d)–(e) (curves, continuation codes), (f)–(g) (O5/O6 or a grid PDE we do not need).

### 3.2 The PRC (`analysis/ode/phase.py`, the same module)

- `phase_sensitivity(cycle, n_points) -> (Θ (n,), Z (n, dim))` by route (c) at the mesh points (from `floquet_periodic_schur`'s factors, with the normalization $\langle Z, f\rangle = 1/T$) and route (e) from the nearest mesh point to each of the `n_points` uniform times (forward solves with `ForwardMode`, a `vmap`). Exact in the sense of the discrete flow, no backward integration, no interpolant. Output in turns: $\Theta_j = t_j/T$, $Z$ scaled by $1/T$.
- `phase_response(cycle, x) -> ∇Θ(x)`: route (d), `jax.grad(asymptotic_phase)` — the response *function* anywhere in the basin; on the cycle a cross-check of (c)+(e) to O3's accuracy.
- Cross-checks in tests, not in the API: (b) by backward integration over $k$ periods on a dense `Dopri8` interpolant of the orbit — Nakao's Appendix 5 recipe (▶ keep as a test-only reference, or expose as `method="adjoint"`?); (a) finite perturbation at a few points.
- ▶ The $Z(p)$ needed by O3's correction (b′) is `phase_sensitivity` at mesh point 0 — computed once per cycle; where to cache it (a field on `Floquet`? recomputed?) is a design point.

## 4. Exact and approximated

Route (a), the one implemented, is a direct integration: the phase is read off the cycle
after $n$ periods, so its error is the solver's global error over those periods plus the
remainder $O(\mu^n)$ of the transient, controlled by $\kappa$ and $n$ (§2.2's paragraph
on the worry that direct integration is noisy); the read-off itself is
`nearest_cycle_time`'s Newton solve. The proposed methods of §3 each state their own
approximation: route (b′) truncates the parametrization at $L = 1$, the parametrization
method at order $L$, and the adjoint route carries the solver's error on the
variational equation. Nothing here is exact beyond the normal forms' closed forms, which
§5 uses as oracles.

## 6. Limits

### 6.1 The phase field

- No isochron *curves*; the field near the almost-phaseless set is reported with its sensitivity, not resolved (that is (e)'s job, not planned).
- One section for the whole basin: a trajectory's $n$-th positive crossing of the *global* hyperplane $\Sigma$ is the $n$-th return near $p$ only once it is close to a convex-enough cycle; a Cassini-like cycle (period-2 map) would miscount — planar convex cycles only, with the period-$k$ detection of O1 as the guard (`k = 1` asserted).

### 6.2 The PRC: limits and consequences

- Planar in the closed form (c′) only; (c), (d), (e) are dimension-free, (c) needs the general periodic Schur for `dim > 2` (ADR-0015 open issue).
- $Z$ is as accurate as the orbit and the segment Jacobians ($10^{-8}$); near the almost-phaseless set, `phase_response` off the cycle inherits O3's sensitivity.
- For training (roadmap M): the PRC error in the first $k$ Fourier modes and the residual $\langle Z, f\rangle - 1/T$ of the *learned* phase are now computable against this $Z$.

### 6.3 O5–O6 and the data items (D2–D3)

Written as each item lands (roadmap Phase E): the parametrization engine of §2.2 (proposed as its own item), then O5 `isostable`, O6 `isostable_response` (Mauroy et al. 2013; Wilson & Moehlis 2016; Shirasaka et al. 2017; Wilson & Ermentrout 2018; Pérez-Cervera et al. 2020), then D2/D3 on the data half.
